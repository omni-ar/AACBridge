The performance of the AACBridge CAP-KVC architecture is evaluated across three primary axes: amortized Time to First Token (TTFT) reduction, background system overhead (CPU/RAM budget), and semantic drift detection accuracy.

\subsection{Important Disclaimer Regarding Clinical Performance}
The fusion model utilized in the AACBridge implementation was trained and evaluated exclusively on simulated sensor data generated by the project dataset pipeline (combining NinaPro DB5 and GazeCapture subsets). Consequently, the results documented in this section represent system integration validation and architectural comparisons. They do not constitute clinical AAC deployment performance metrics, which remain a target for future physical hardware integration trials.

\subsection{Fusion Architecture Ablation and Model Selection}
\label{subsec:fusion_results}

Following the resolution of the label leakage anomaly (which necessitated reducing the dataset gaze vector dimension from 6 to 5), the ablation study yielded conclusive results regarding the optimal multimodal aggregation strategy. 

The evaluation demonstrated that the \textbf{Late Fusion} architecture achieved an Accuracy of 0.9967 and an F1 score of 0.9963. In contrast, the more computationally heavy \textbf{Cross-Attention} architecture achieved an Accuracy of 0.9467 and an F1 score of 0.9437. 

Because the predefined architectural threshold demanded that cross-attention exceed late fusion by a $>3\%$ F1 margin to justify its increased footprint and latency, cross-attention was definitively rejected. Late Fusion was formally selected as the production intent classification backbone.

\subsection{EMG TFLite Export Validation}
\label{subsec:tflite_validation}

To guarantee that the mathematical fidelity of the selected Late Fusion architecture was preserved across the JNI and mobile compilation boundaries, the exported PyTorch model was subjected to a rigorous quantization and export pipeline. The transition from PyTorch $\rightarrow$ ONNX $\rightarrow$ TFLite (INT8) was verified using localized tensor matching. The deployment pipeline achieved a confirmed \texttt{assert\_allclose(atol=1e-3)} passing status, recording a maximum absolute error of $9.30 \times 10^{-4}$ between the PyTorch float32 baseline and the on-device TFLite model. This confirms the stability of the model payload prior to runtime intent generation.
